02
The Chianti Hills Fri Jul 31 – Sun Aug 2 · 2 nights

Your own 600-year-old wine estate in the heart of Chianti

You're not just visiting Chianti — you're living on a working Chianti Classico estate for two nights. Which means the plan flips: the wine, the pool and the sunset are already home. Use the days for a couple of great meals and one lazy drive, and let Vignamaggio do the rest.

🍇
Your base
Fri 31 Jul→ Sun 2 Aug · 2 nights

Villa Vignamaggio

Greve in Chianti · ~33 km / 1 hr south of Florence

A wine estate producing Chianti Classico since 1404, set on 400 hectares between Greve and Panzano — and, by legend, the birthplace of Lisa Gherardini, the woman behind the Mona Lisa. (Branagh filmed Much Ado About Nothing in these gardens.) On-site tastings at La Fattoria, guest-only pools, and formal Renaissance gardens to wander at golden hour.

🍷 On-site tastings🏊 Guest pools🎾 Tennis🐴 Horse riding🚲 Bikes🌿 Renaissance gardens

03
Porto Ercole · Monte Argentario Mon Aug 3 – Wed Aug 5 · 2 nights

Tuscany's most stylish stretch of coast — forts, coves and a yacht-filled bay

Two nights on the Monte Argentario promontory: a near-island of Spanish star-forts, hidden turquoise coves, a glossy marina, and the wild Maremma coast at your back. This is where Rome's smart set summers — and where Caravaggio washed ashore for his final act in 1610. With one full day here, pick your battles and swim well.

The drive · Mon Florence → Porto Ercole ~2 hr 40 · 190 km, all road No ferry this leg — the car just rolls south down the coast

A 6 km arc of golden sand backed by a centuries-old pine forest on the sandbar linking Argentario to the mainland. Real shade, calm water, Blue Flag every year — the easy, do-nothing beach day. A flat cycle path runs the whole reserve.

🏖 sand + shade⏱ ~10 min from town

History

Caravaggio's last stop

The great painter died here on 18 July 1610, on the run from a murder charge in Rome. The Church of Sant'Erasmo in the old town anchors the enduring mystery of where he's buried — a strange, moving footnote to a swim-and-sun week.

Two nights on the island where the emperor spent his exile — based in Magazzini, an old fishing hamlet looking across the Gulf of Portoferraio at the town lights. You get one full island day and two golden evenings: swim it, sail it, and eat very well.

Quiet, local, and perfectly placed: town is 10 minutes one way, the beaches of Bagnaia and Nisporto minutes the other.

Good to know

The practical stuff, in one place

Flights, beds and the car are already sorted — these are the small things that keep a drive-and-ferry trip smooth.

🚗Driving in Italy

  • ZTL zones: Florence & Rome city centres are camera-enforced no-drive areas. Park outside and walk, or you'll get fined by mail weeks later.
  • Autostrada tolls: keep some cash/card handy for the toll booths on the A1.
  • IDP: an International Driving Permit alongside your licence is technically required — worth having.

⛴The Elba ferry

  • Book the car on early, both ways. Piombino ⇄ Portoferraio, ~1 hr, run by Moby, Toremar & Blu Navy — August crossings fill up.
  • Arrive ~1 hr before departure when travelling with a vehicle.
  • Friday morning: take an early boat — Rome is still ~3 hrs of driving after you land at Piombino.

🏖Coast & coves

  • Feniglia is the easy sandy beach — but the car park fills early in August, so arrive in the morning.
  • The prettiest coves (like Cala del Gesso) need a short scramble or a boat — pack water shoes for the pebbles.
  • Book beach clubs and a boat rental ahead — this is the yacht crowd's coast in high summer.

🧳Pack for it

  • Swimwear ×2, reef-safe sunscreen, and your own snorkel mask — the Argentario and Elba coves reward it.
  • Water shoes: many of the best beaches are pebble, not sand.
  • One smart-casual outfit each for a nice Porto Ercole or Roman dinner; otherwise light and breathable.
  • EU Type F/L adapter and a power bank for beach days.

📶Money & phone

  • Grab an eSIM before you fly — data works the moment you land.
  • Cards are widely accepted, but carry cash for small trattorias, markets and beach kiosks (some are cash-only).
  • Aperitivo is ~6–8pm; kitchens open late (dinner from ~8pm). Adjust your clock south.
